# Frontier-6

Date: 2026-04-24

This report follows `frontier-5`.

The remaining pro-DER hope after `frontier-5` was narrow:

> maybe exact `11`- and `12`-byte DER are still interesting, but only once the witness deletes more tokens and the interaction becomes genuinely higher-order.

That is the right next question because `frontier-5` only probed ordered `2`-of-`4`.

If short DER needed three-way interactions to wake up, the pairwise flatness in `frontier-5` would have been misleading.

The tool for this step is `tools/der_higher_order_frontier.py`.

## Result 1: exact `11`- and `12`-byte DER stayed flat at ordered `3`-of-`5`

The tool runs random higher-order probes on exact-length DER-valid signatures:

- exact payload lengths `11` and `12`
- random `5`-token families
- ordered `3`-of-`5` deletion

Results:

| Surface | Trials | Unordered subset baseline | Best unique ordered outcomes | Best unordered-subset multiplicity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| exact `11`-byte DER | `3000` | `10` | `10` | `1` |
| exact `12`-byte DER | `3000` | `10` | `10` | `1` |

So the first higher-order lift produced no gain at all.

The best families stayed exactly on the plain subset baseline.

That matters because if useful overlap were present but merely higher-order, `3`-of-`5` was the first reasonable place to expect it.

## Result 2: exact `11`- and `12`-byte DER also stayed flat at ordered `3`-of-`6`

The tool then pushes one more step:

- random `6`-token families
- ordered `3`-of-`6` deletion

Results:

| Surface | Trials | Unordered subset baseline | Best unique ordered outcomes | Best unordered-subset multiplicity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| exact `11`-byte DER | `2000` | `20` | `20` | `1` |
| exact `12`-byte DER | `2000` | `20` | `20` | `1` |

So even after increasing both family size and deletion order, short exact DER still behaves like plain subset selection.

This is a stronger negative result than `frontier-5`, not just a repeat:

- `frontier-5`: pairwise probe stayed flat
- `frontier-6`: higher-order probe still stayed flat

## Result 3: motif-heavy exhaustive pools also stayed flat under higher-order search

Random search can still miss structured families, so the tool also exhaustively checks two motif-heavy pools under ordered `3`-of-`5` deletion:

1. `len11_motif_pool_m5_t3`
2. `len12_motif_pool_m5_t3`

Each pool has `8` candidates.

The tool checks:

- every `5`-token family from the pool
- every script order for that family
- ordered `3`-of-`5` deletion

Results:

| Pool | Unordered subset baseline | Best unique ordered outcomes | Best unordered-subset multiplicity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `len11_motif_pool_m5_t3` | `10` | `10` | `1` |
| `len12_motif_pool_m5_t3` | `10` | `10` | `1` |

So even the first obvious structured short-DER motifs remain exactly subset-like under higher-order deletion.

## Result 4: this materially weakens the short-DER path

The cumulative picture is now:

- minimal `9`-byte DER is structurally dead
- exact `10`-byte DER is structurally dead
- exact `11`- and `12`-byte DER stayed flat under ordered `2`-of-`4`
- exact `11`- and `12`-byte DER also stayed flat under ordered `3`-of-`5` and `3`-of-`6`
- motif-heavy exhaustive pools for exact `11` and `12` also stayed flat

That is enough to shift the burden of proof.

Short DER-shaped selectable blobs no longer look like a promising hidden frontier that just needs a better local search.

They now look exhausted through exact length `12`, at least for the first meaningful overlap regimes.

## What is left

The next worthwhile step is no longer another short-DER search.

The next plausible options are:

1. materially longer DER surfaces, where the byte budget starts to hurt but the overlap geometry may finally change;
2. a different valid selectable blob family entirely, instead of continuing to privilege DER-shaped blobs;
3. a new verification surface that does not rely on overlap-coded `FindAndDelete` as the main entropy source.

That is where the repo should go next.
